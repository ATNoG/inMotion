\begin{abstract}
    Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) enables the opportunistic reuse of existing wireless infrastructure, allowing commodity Wi-Fi access points to passively classify passenger mobility dynamics in public transport without dedicated radar or optical sensors. While Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) sequences are natively accessible across off-the-shelf devices, non-stationary multi-device interference and multipath fading severely limit classical supervised classifiers, which previously reached a Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) of 0.756 on initial collection benchmarks. To overcome these representational limitations, this paper introduces self-supervised Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) world models, a supervised baseline regularized with Sliced Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) on the empirical characteristic function, and selective state-space architectures trained on a primary 3,511-sample transit dataset. On this dataset, the leading self-supervised model, Time-Series JEPA (TS-JEPA), achieves a single-model MCC of 0.9194, while a 16-member stacked ensemble attains 0.9232 (0.9563 on multi-device noise) and a compact three-member self-supervised subset reaches 0.9160 with only 3.42 million parameters. Crucially, self-supervised representations generalize robustly across distinct collection regimes where supervised baselines experience catastrophic collapse (dropping from 0.8858 to 0.4872), and game-theoretic Shapley additive attribution coupled with empirical pruning confirms that a compact 4-channel base subset retains 99.4\% of peak classification performance (0.9143 vs. 0.9194 MCC) while eliminating 77.8\% of front-end feature extraction for lightweight edge execution.
\end{abstract}
