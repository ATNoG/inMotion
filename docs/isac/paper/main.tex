\begin{abstract}
    Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) enables the opportunistic reuse of existing wireless infrastructure, allowing commodity Wi-Fi access points to passively classify passenger mobility dynamics in public transport without dedicated radar or optical sensors. While Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) sequences are natively accessible across off-the-shelf devices, non-stationary multi-device interference and multipath fading severely limit classical supervised classifiers, which previously reached a Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) of 0.756 on initial collection benchmarks. To overcome these representational limitations, this paper introduces self-supervised Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) world models, a characteristic-function regularized supervised baseline (SIGReg), and selective state-space architectures trained on an extended 3,511-sample transit dataset. On this extended collection, the leading self-supervised model, TS-JEPA, achieves an MCC of 0.9194, while a 16-member stacked ensemble attains 0.9232 and a compact three-member Pareto subset reaches 0.8904 with only 1.26 million parameters. Crucially, self-supervised representations generalize robustly across distinct collection regimes where supervised baselines experience catastrophic collapse, and feature permutation analysis confirms that a pruned five-channel subset retains 95\% of discriminative capacity for lightweight edge execution.
\end{abstract}
