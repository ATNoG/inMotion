\section{Conclusion}

This paper demonstrated opportunistic Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) for passenger movement classification in public transport using commodity Wi-Fi infrastructure. By passively capturing standard \ac{RSSI} sequences without dedicated radar or firmware modifications, self-supervised \ac{JEPA} world models learn environment-invariant representations of passenger mobility. On the primary 3,511-sample dataset, \ac{TS-JEPA} achieves a single-model \ac{MCC} of 0.9194, while a 16-member calibrated stacked ensemble reaches 0.9232 (0.9563 on multi-device noise) and a compact 3-member self-supervised subset attains 0.9160 with 3.42~M parameters.

A central insight is the contrast in cross-environment transferability between self-supervised and supervised sequence modeling. While self-supervised world models exhibit positive transfer across collection regimes (+0.029 to +0.042 \ac{MCC}), supervised regularized baselines suffer representational collapse. Furthermore, game-theoretic \ac{SHAP} attribution and empirical pruning confirm that a compact 4-channel base subset preserves 99.4\% of peak classification performance (0.9143 vs. 0.9194 \ac{MCC}), enabling a 4.5-fold front-end computational reduction for real-time edge access points.

These findings confirm that commodity Wi-Fi access points can effectively serve as dual-functional nodes for intelligent transportation systems. Future work will explore multi-access-point spatial diversity, long-horizon route modeling, and online adaptation for seasonal radio drift. Open-source models and reproducibility scripts are available at \cite{githubrepo}.
