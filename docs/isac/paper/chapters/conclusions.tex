\section{Conclusion}

This paper demonstrated opportunistic Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) for passenger movement classification in public transport using commodity Wi-Fi infrastructure. Passively capturing standard \ac{RSSI} sequences without dedicated radar or firmware modifications, self-supervised \ac{JEPA} world models learn environment-invariant representations of passenger mobility. On the primary 3,511-sample dataset, \ac{TS-JEPA} achieves a single-model \ac{MCC} of 0.9194, while a 16-member calibrated stacked ensemble reaches 0.9232 (0.9563 on multi-device noise) and a compact 3-member self-supervised subset attains 0.9160 with 3.42~M parameters.

A central insight is the contrast in cross-environment transferability between self-supervised and supervised modeling. While self-supervised world models exhibit positive transfer across collection regimes (+0.029 to +0.042 \ac{MCC}), supervised regularized baselines suffer representational collapse. Furthermore, game-theoretic \ac{SHAP} attribution and controlled feature ablation reveal that cross-domain interaction between kinematic and spectral descriptors drives multi-channel models, while dedicated lightweight models like LeJEPA deliver near-peak performance (0.9143 \ac{MCC}) directly over 4 base kinematic channels for edge execution. These findings establish that commodity access points can effectively serve as dual-functional sensing nodes in intelligent transit networks. Future work will explore cooperative multi-access-point spatial diversity and online continual adaptation for seasonal radio drift.

Furthermore, to address the privacy vulnerabilities identified in continuous radio sensing, future investigations will develop self-adaptive governance mechanisms for elastic privacy. Originally formulated for continuous IoT and personal sensor telemetry \cite{silva2026elastic}, the elastic privacy paradigm dynamically modulates the trade-off between privacy protection and sensing utility according to operational context. In vehicular opportunistic ISAC, this framework can be adapted to throttle sensing resolution based on vehicle motion state: maintaining fine-grained temporal resolution only at doorway thresholds during dwell stops to resolve boarding and alighting transitions, while dynamically coarsening spatial precision (e.g., binary cabin presence rather than continuous tracking) and adaptively quantizing latent representations during inter-stop cruising. Such context-driven elastic governance would guarantee verifiable privacy thresholds against longitudinal machine-learning profiling without compromising the macro-level counting fidelity required for fleet optimization.
