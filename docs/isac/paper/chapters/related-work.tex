\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Passive Wi-Fi Sensing and Passenger Monitoring}
Automatic passenger counting is essential in public transit. While video-based \ac{APC} systems deploy cameras for neural tracking \cite{pronello2023evaluating}, they require dedicated hardware and raise severe privacy concerns. In contrast, Wi-Fi sensing passively monitors radio emissions from passenger devices at zero hardware expense. While Channel State Information (CSI) provides subcarrier resolution for cabin monitoring \cite{wang2024csi}, it requires custom firmware. The \ac{RSSI}, by contrast, is ubiquitous across standard routers \cite{ahmad2024wifisensing}, enabling localization \cite{singh2021rssisurvey}, probe occupancy counting \cite{nitti2020iabacus}, signature tracking \cite{myrvoll2017counting}, and activity classification \cite{sigg2014telepathic}.

\subsection{Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC)}
Integrated sensing and communications leverages cellular and wireless infrastructure as dual-functional sensing nodes \cite{liu2022isac,gonzalezprelcic2024isac}, where machine learning extracts physical environment states from ambient signals \cite{luong2025mlisac}. Our work adopts this opportunistic paradigm, classifying transit states from \ac{RSSI} time series using standard onboard access points without dedicated radar units.

\subsection{Sequence Modeling and World Models}
Sequence modeling has advanced through selective state spaces (Mamba \cite{gu2023mamba}, Mamba-3 \cite{gu2026mamba3}) and self-supervised joint-embedding architectures (I-JEPA \cite{assran2023ijepa}, LeJEPA \cite{balestriero2025lejepa}). Recent extensions adapt JEPAs to tabular and temporal data \cite{thimonier2024tjepa,tsjepa2024,cfjepa2026}, offering strong potential for radio sensing where labeled traces are scarce \cite{radwan2025sslwifi}. While prior benchmarks achieved 0.756 \ac{MCC} via Gaussian Processes \cite{ribeiro2026eucnc}, this work is the first to deploy self-supervised world models for opportunistic Wi-Fi ISAC in public transit.

\subsection{Security, Privacy, and Profiling in Wireless Sensing}
Although passive radio sensing avoids recording visual or acoustic biometrics, opportunistic wireless sensing introduces distinct privacy vulnerabilities. Even when mobile operating systems enforce MAC address randomization, physical-layer radio characteristics, transmission burst timings, and sequential \ac{RSSI} trajectories remain susceptible to device fingerprinting and behavioral tracking. Recent studies underscore that standard obfuscation techniques can be systematically defeated by machine-learning-driven profiling. Specifically, Silva et al. \cite{silva2025iknow} demonstrated that user profiling models can extract consistent behavioral patterns and privacy preferences from observational interaction data, effectively re-identifying users despite privacy-preserving software abstractions. Furthermore, investigating the efficacy of differential privacy against profiling attacks, Silva et al. \cite{silva2025evaluatingdp} established that even mathematically grounded noise addition can be circumvented when machine-learning classifiers exploit aggregate temporal correlations and structural invariants. These findings highlight that opportunistic ISAC cannot rely solely on identifier anonymization or naive perturbation, requiring conscious architectural isolation and privacy-preserving representation learning.
