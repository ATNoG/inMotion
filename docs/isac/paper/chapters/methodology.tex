\section{Materials and Methods}

We formulate passenger movement classification as a four-class time-series task over short Wi-Fi \ac{RSSI} windows. This section details the physical collection environment, the 18-channel feature pipeline, the self-supervised world models, the supervised baselines, and the calibrated ensemble stacking framework.

\subsection{Physical Sensing Setup and Dataset Composition}
\label{sec:data}
\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{images/rssi_trajectory.pdf}
    \caption{Mean RSSI trajectory per passenger movement class ($\pm 1\,\sigma$ shaded variance band) across 10-second observation windows on the 3,511-sample collection.}
    \label{fig:rssi_trajectory}
\end{figure}

The physical sensing environment comprises two spatial zones emulating public transport interactions as established in \cite{ribeiro2026eucnc}: Zone~A denotes the interior passenger cabin (an enclosed room simulating a vehicle interior with a Wi-Fi access point adjacent to the doorway), and Zone~B denotes the exterior platform or bus stop (the adjacent corridor). An onboard passive sniffer records 802.11 transmissions from passenger mobile devices. Spatial transitions define four mutually exclusive movement classes: internal transit (AA, Zone~A $\to$ Zone~A), alighting (AB, Zone~A $\to$ Zone~B), boarding (BA, Zone~B $\to$ Zone~A), and platform waiting (BB, Zone~B $\to$ Zone~B).

Observations consist of 10-second \ac{RSSI} sequences sampled at 1~Hz ($T=10$, $t \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ seconds). As depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:rssi_trajectory}, each class exhibits a characteristic radio attenuation profile: internal transit (AA) maintains a high, stable signal ($\approx -40\,\text{dBm}$), alighting (AB) exhibits a monotonic decline from $-42\,\text{dBm}$ to $-52\,\text{dBm}$, boarding (BA) rises from $-51\,\text{dBm}$ to $-43\,\text{dBm}$, and platform waiting (BB) remains attenuated at $-52\,\text{dBm}$. The shaded $\pm 1\,\sigma$ variance bands capture multi-device concurrent interference while confirming the physical separability of the mobility states.

The dataset contains 3,511 labeled sequences, divided into 160 single-device calibration traces (pure scenario, 40 per class) and 3,351 multi-device concurrent interference traces (noise scenario, $\approx 840$ per class). Data collection follows the controlled indoor methodology of \cite{ribeiro2026eucnc}, scaling the hardware diversity from 4 smartphones in \cite{ribeiro2026eucnc} to 7 distinct smartphone models across multiple vendors and Android releases. The 3,511-sample benchmark is a strict superset of the 2,251-sample collection in \cite{matos2026icaisf} under identical sensing protocols: it retains all eight devices of the earlier campaign together with their traces, and adds five further devices. Consequently, 74\% of the expanded collection originates from devices already represented in the prior one, and comparisons between the two quantify stability under collection expansion rather than transfer to an independent environment.

Data is partitioned with an 80/20 class-stratified split, holding out 12.5\% of training data for validation ($\approx 10\%$ of total data). Training folds are augmented within folds using class-conditioned Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 0.05$) and intra-class Mixup ($\alpha = 0.4$), yielding 35,839 training samples with zero split contamination. Model selection is based on validation \ac{MCC}, and the test split is evaluated once for final reporting.

\subsection{18-Channel Rich Feature Pipeline}

To capture the temporal, statistical, and spectral dynamics of radio attenuation, we construct an 18-channel feature hierarchy from raw \ac{RSSI} $r = [r_1, \dots, r_{10}]$. The pipeline comprises four functional domains: kinematics and deviation (4 channels: raw $r_t$, velocity $\Delta_t = r_t - r_{t-1}$, acceleration $\Delta^2_t = \Delta_t - \Delta_{t-1}$, and window deviation $d_t = r_t - \mu_w$); rolling statistical moments (4 channels: rolling mean $\mu_{t,w}$, standard deviation $\sigma_{t,w}$, dynamic range $R_{t,w}$, skewness $\gamma_{t,w}$, kurtosis $\kappa_{t,w}$, and velocity sign changes); spectral descriptors (4 channels: FFT DC power $\text{band\_dc}$, AC residual energy $\text{band\_ac}$, and peak location index); and time-frequency STFT sub-bands (4 channels: Hann-windowed sub-bands $\text{stft\_b0}$ through $\text{stft\_b3}$ capturing micro-Doppler motion). The plain baseline uses only the 4 kinematic channels, whereas the rich pipeline uses all 18 channels.

\subsection{Self-Supervised World Models}

All \ac{JEPA} models learn invariant latent dynamics by predicting representations of masked or future temporal regions. An online context encoder $f_\theta$ is updated via gradient descent, a target encoder $g_\xi$ is updated via \ac{EMA} ($\xi \leftarrow \tau \xi + (1-\tau)\theta$), and a latent predictor $h_\psi$ minimizes the L2 prediction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{JEPA}} = \| h_\psi(f_\theta(x_c)) - \text{sg}(g_\xi(x_t)) \|_2^2$, where $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ is the stop-gradient operator. We evaluate four distinct formulations, whose architectural parameters and pretraining settings are detailed in Table~\ref{tab:arch_specs}:

\textbf{T-JEPA} \cite{thimonier2024tjepa} applies per-timestep channel tokenization with learned feature embeddings and two learnable \texttt{[REG]} tokens (prepended to context and target to prevent representational collapse), applying contiguous 30\% temporal masks.
\textbf{Time-Series JEPA (\ac{TS-JEPA})} \cite{tsjepa2024} utilizes a 1D convolutional patch tokenizer (patch size two) with patch masking annealed from 0.4 to 0.7 across training.
\textbf{LeJEPA} \cite{balestriero2025lejepa} executes next-step latent prediction without an \ac{EMA} target, stabilized via \ac{SIGReg} on the empirical characteristic function of latents.
\textbf{CF-JEPA} \cite{cfjepa2026} executes mask-free multi-horizon prediction across horizons $k \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ from context crops with an \ac{EMA} target.

Pretraining runs for 500 epochs with AdamW, cosine annealing, gradient clipping at 1.0, and target momentum schedules shown in Table~\ref{tab:arch_specs}. Fine-tuning employs progressive unfreezing (frozen backbone for 25\% of epochs, then unfrozen at 0.1 learning rate with Mixup $\alpha = 0.4$ and label smoothing $\varepsilon = 0.1$). All fine-tuned models share a learned-attention pooling head mapping encoder latents through a two-layer MLP with dropout (0.3) to 4-class logits.

\subsection{Supervised Classifiers and State-Space Baselines}

\textbf{\ac{SIGReg}} \cite{balestriero2025lejepa} is a 3-block 1D convolutional network (192 filters) with global pooling and 128-unit projection, regularized by \ac{SIGReg} ($\lambda = 0.01$) over 256 random projections (0.697~M parameters, 4 channels).
\textbf{Mamba-3 Sequence Models} \cite{gu2026mamba3} employ selective state spaces \cite{gu2023mamba} (state dim 16, rank-4 MIMO) with four front-ends: \ac{CNN} (0.945~M), \ac{TCN} \cite{bai2018tcn} (0.843~M), Transformer (0.842~M), and multi-view fusion (1.701~M) over 4 channels.
\textbf{DeepStack} is a two-stage stacked ensemble \cite{wolpert1992stacked} combining ten base models via Level-2 networks and a DeepMetaMLP (43.576~M parameters). Supervised sequence baselines are tuned via Optuna \cite{optuna2019} with \ac{TPE} over 50 trials per model.

\subsection{Calibrated Stacking and Pareto Optimization}

To exploit complementary representations across model families while controlling edge complexity, we construct a calibrated stacking framework. Uncalibrated softmax outputs can produce overconfident misclassifications under noise. We therefore temperature-calibrate the raw logits $z_m$ of each candidate model $m$ by optimizing a scalar temperature parameter $T_m > 0$ on validation data to minimize Negative Log-Likelihood (\ac{NLL}) \cite{guo2017calibration}:
\begin{equation}
    p_m = \text{softmax}(z_m / T_m).
\end{equation}
The calibrated class probability vectors are concatenated into a meta-feature vector $\mathbf{u} = [p_1^\top, \dots, p_M^\top]^\top$ and passed to a multinomial logistic regression meta-learner \cite{wolpert1992stacked} with L2 regularization penalty $C \in [0.005, 10.0]$.

Hyperparameter optimization for model selection and ensemble pruning is formulated as a bi-objective search over the Pareto frontier balancing classification \ac{MCC} against total parameter count. Starting from an empty ensemble, a greedy Pareto algorithm iteratively evaluates candidate additions, selecting model subsets that maximize incremental \ac{MCC} gain per added parameter. This directly yields minimal-footprint configurations tailored for edge devices.

\subsection{Channel-Level Interpretability \& Attribution}

To quantify the relative contribution of each channel within the 18-channel hierarchy, we employ Shapley Additive exPlanations (\ac{SHAP}) \cite{lundberg2017shap}. Grounded in cooperative game theory, \ac{SHAP} evaluates the marginal contribution of each feature channel $j$ across all possible feature subsets (coalitions) $S \subseteq F \setminus \{j\}$:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_j(x) = \sum_{S \subseteq F \setminus \{j\}} \frac{|S|!(|F| - |S| - 1)!}{|F|!} \left[ f(S \cup \{j\}) - f(S) \right],
\end{equation}
where $F$ denotes the set of $M=18$ feature channels, and $f(S)$ denotes model predictions conditioned on coalition $S$ with absent features evaluated against empirical background distributions. The resulting attributions satisfy the efficiency axiom $\sum_{j=1}^{|F|} \phi_j(x) = f(x) - \mathbb{E}[f(x)]$, providing an exact decomposition of model predictions across input channels.
