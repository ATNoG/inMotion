\section{Introduction}

Urban public transportation networks require continuous passenger mobility monitoring to optimize fleet dispatching and route occupancy. While conventional automated passenger counting relies on optical cameras or infrared beams, these solutions incur substantial maintenance costs and raise privacy concerns. In contrast, opportunistic Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) transforms standard wireless infrastructure into dual-functional sensing nodes without dedicated radar hardware. Commodity onboard Wi-Fi access points passively capture \ac{RSSI} streams from client devices during routine network exchanges, enabling non-intrusive, privacy-preserving tracking at zero incremental hardware expense.

In transit monitoring, mobility is cast as classifying four operational states: internal vehicle transit, alighting transitions, boarding events, and stationary platform waiting. Prior research established an initial benchmark of 2,251 \ac{RSSI} samples \cite{matos2026icaisf}, where a Gaussian Process achieved an \ac{MCC} of 0.756 \cite{ribeiro2026eucnc}. However, supervised neural networks face severe limitations in vehicular radio environments. \ac{RSSI} streams suffer from multipath fading and cross-traffic interference from concurrent devices. Supervised classifiers trained on limited annotations tend to memorize antenna-specific interference artifacts rather than underlying transit kinetics, resulting in brittle boundaries under environmental drift.

To overcome the fragility of supervised radio modeling, self-supervised learning via \ac{JEPA} world models provides a compelling alternative. Rather than reconstructing noisy high-frequency signals, \ac{JEPA} models predict abstract representations in a latent embedding space, capturing physical motion constraints while discarding non-semantic radio noise.

In this paper, we address these challenges through four contributions. First, we formulate passenger mobility sensing within a self-supervised world model framework, deploying four distinct \ac{JEPA} architectures (\ac{TS-JEPA}, LeJEPA, CF-JEPA, T-JEPA) alongside selective state-space models and regularized baselines. Second, we introduce a primary 3,511-sample transit dataset, establishing that self-supervised world models generalize across collection regimes (+0.042 \ac{MCC}), whereas supervised baselines suffer collapse. Third, we construct an 18-channel hierarchy, evaluate attribution via \ac{SHAP}, and demonstrate through architecture-controlled ablation that cross-domain feature interaction drives multi-channel tokenizers, while dedicated compact models (LeJEPA at 0.566~M parameters) achieve 0.9143 \ac{MCC} directly over 4 base channels. Fourth, we develop temperature-calibrated ensembles, achieving a peak \ac{MCC} of 0.9232 and isolating a compact three-member self-supervised ensemble delivering 0.9160 \ac{MCC} with 3.42~M parameters.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~II reviews related work. Section~III details the setup, feature hierarchy, \ac{JEPA} world models, and calibrated stacking. Section~IV analyzes empirical classification benchmarks, cross-collection transfer, feature attribution, and edge trade-offs. Finally, Section~V concludes the paper.
